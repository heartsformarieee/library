VEIL OF FORKS
A Twilight alternate-universe fanfiction
by Elias

Bella Swan does not exist in this continuity. Marie comes to Forks instead.

Edward Cullen becomes fascinated with the one mind he cannot hear. Unfortunately for him, Marie is considerably less fascinated with Edward Cullen.

CHAPTER 1 — THE NEW GIRL

Forks looked permanently unfinished, as though somebody had sketched a town in charcoal and then left it outside in the rain.

Marie disliked it immediately.

By eight-thirty Monday morning, Forks High School knew three things about her: she was new, her hair was naturally ginger, and she had no interest in answering the same six questions from twenty different people.

Jessica Stanley adopted her anyway.

At lunch, Jessica pointed toward the far table.

“The Cullens.”

Marie looked because Jessica clearly expected her to.

Five impossibly beautiful students sat together. Rosalie looked like an advertisement for expensive perfume. Emmett was enormous. Alice looked like a particularly elegant pixie. Jasper appeared mildly tortured by the existence of cafeterias.

The last one was staring at Marie.

Edward Cullen.

His expression was not flirtatious. It was furious, bewildered and intensely focused all at once.

Marie met his gaze for two seconds.

Then she returned to her chips.

Across the room, Edward Cullen experienced something very close to an existential crisis.

He could hear Jessica wondering whether Marie thought he was attractive. Mike Newton was already planning how to introduce himself. Angela was wondering whether Marie needed help finding her next classroom.

Marie herself gave him nothing.

Silence.

He pushed outward with his gift.

Nothing.

For ninety years the minds around him had been open windows.

Hers was a locked door.

Marie laughed at something Jessica said.

Edward stared.

Alice nudged him. “You’re being creepy.”

“I can’t hear her.”

Alice went still.

Marie glanced over again, caught Edward staring for the second time, and visibly rolled her eyes.

Edward had never been more interested in another person in his life.

Marie had already forgotten about him.

That irritated him far more than it should have.

CHAPTER 2 — LAB PARTNERS

The universe, Edward decided, had a vicious sense of humour.

“Cullen. Marie. Table three,” Mr. Banner announced.

Marie sat beside Edward without ceremony and opened the worksheet.

Edward had spent the entire morning preparing himself for her scent. He had hunted. He had rehearsed breathing patterns. He had promised Carlisle that if control became difficult, he would leave.

He had not prepared for Marie to say, “Move your elbow. You’re on my paper.”

Edward moved it.

She began identifying slides.

“Prophase.”

“Yes.”

“Metaphase.”

“Yes.”

She stopped. “Are you going to contribute anything besides agreeing with me?”

“I was going to let you finish.”

“How charitable.”

Edward almost smiled.

He tried to hear her again.

Nothing.

“What?” Marie asked.

“You’re difficult to read.”

“That sounds like a you problem.”

It was.

A spectacular one.

When the bell rang, Marie left before Edward could manufacture another reason to speak to her.

He sat alone for several seconds.

He knew the rhythm of her heartbeat already.

He knew she preferred the window seat.

He knew she tapped her pen twice before writing an answer.

He knew she smelled like something designed specifically to destroy his self-control.

He did not know what she thought of him.

That absence gnawed at him.

By the time he reached the parking lot, he had made a decision.

He would learn.

CHAPTER 3 — BACKGROUND NOISE

Edward listened to everybody except Marie.

It was the nearest thing he had to cheating.

Jessica thought Marie was funny but intimidating. Angela liked her. Mike thought she was pretty. Tyler planned to invite her somewhere after school.

Edward disliked Tyler's plan instantly.

He disliked himself for disliking it a moment later.

Marie stood at her locker while Tyler leaned against the one beside it.

“There’s a group going into Port Angeles Friday. You should come.”

“Maybe.”

Maybe.

Edward hated the word.

He walked past them.

Marie did not look at him.

That was becoming ridiculous.

People always looked at him. Humans noticed the Cullens whether they wanted to or not. Their beauty was part camouflage, part lure.

Marie seemed immune to the social gravity of Edward Cullen.

He spent history listening to Tyler imagine Friday.

By lunch, Alice had had enough.

“You’re stalking her through other people's thoughts.”

“I am not stalking anyone.”

“You know what she bought from the vending machine.”

Edward looked at her.

Alice grinned. “Exactly.”

Across the cafeteria, Marie opened a Sprite.

Edward looked away before Alice could become more insufferable.

CHAPTER 4 — THE FIRST NIGHT

Edward went to Marie's house at 1:14 a.m.

He told himself it was about her scent.

That explanation survived approximately four minutes.

He stood beyond the tree line where the rain softened every sound except the one he had come to hear.

Her heartbeat.

Through a gap in the curtains he could see her asleep beneath a blanket.

He should leave.

Instead, he remained until dawn.

There was something obscene about the peace of it. Marie could not ignore him while asleep because she did not know he was there. He did not have to wonder what she thought. He could simply exist near her silence.

At 5:42 she shifted in bed and muttered something unintelligible.

Edward smiled before realizing he was smiling.

He vanished before the house woke.

At breakfast, Rosalie looked at his damp jacket.

“You’re pathetic.”

Emmett looked delighted. “Oh, this is going to be fun.”

Edward ignored both of them.

Alice said nothing.

Her expression was worse.

She had seen him returning the next night.

And the night after that.

CHAPTER 5 — NOT IMPRESSED

Edward attempted charm.

It went badly.

“You seem determined to avoid me,” he said after biology.

Marie zipped her bag. “I wasn't aware I was supposed to schedule time for you.”

Edward blinked.

“No.”

“Good.”

She walked away.

He caught up effortlessly. “Did I offend you?”

“No.”

“Then why—”

Marie stopped.

“Edward, you’re attractive. Congratulations. So are half the people on television. I don't know you.”

Then she continued toward English.

Edward remained in the hallway.

He had been rejected before. That was not extraordinary.

What bothered him was that Marie had not rejected him.

Rejection required interest first.

She simply did not care.

He spent the next class staring at the clock.

CHAPTER 6 — PORT ANGELES

Marie went to Port Angeles with Jessica and Angela.

Edward knew because Jessica had been thinking about it all day.

He did not follow them.

For twenty-three minutes.

Then he followed them.

He remained several streets away, furious with himself and unwilling to turn around.

When Marie separated from the others near a bookshop, Edward heard four men notice her.

Their thoughts changed his mood instantly.

He found her in an alley before they reached her.

The Volvo stopped hard against the curb.

“Get in.”

Marie stared through the passenger window. “What?”

“Marie. Get in the car.”

Something in his face made her obey.

Edward drove too fast.

His hands crushed the steering wheel.

Marie watched him. “Are you angry at me?”

“No.”

“Then stop acting like I keyed your car.”

Edward exhaled.

“I wanted to kill them.”

Silence.

Marie looked back through the window.

“That's not normal.”

“I know.”

“You barely know me.”

“I know.”

That was the part Edward could not explain.

CHAPTER 7 — QUESTIONS

At the restaurant, Marie demanded answers.

Edward gave her carefully edited ones.

“How did you know where I was?”

“I was nearby.”

“Lie.”

Edward's eyebrows rose.

“You appeared in the exact alley where four men cornered me.”

“I have good instincts.”

“Another lie.”

He smiled despite himself. “You’re very suspicious.”

“You’re very suspicious.”

Marie ate while Edward pretended the food in front of him served a purpose.

She noticed he never touched it.

She noticed his eyes changed colour.

She noticed his skin was cold when their hands accidentally brushed.

Edward watched the conclusions gather behind a mind he could not enter.

It was torture.

“Tell me what you're thinking.”

“No.”

“Why not?”

“Because you keep asking.”

Edward stared.

Marie smiled sweetly and took another chip.

He understood then that she had discovered exactly how much her silence bothered him.

Worse, she found it funny.

CHAPTER 8 — THE SEARCH

Marie searched vampire legends online.

Edward watched from the tree outside her bedroom.

He knew he had crossed a line.

He had crossed several by then.

The blue light from her laptop reflected across her face as she moved through myths: cold ones, blood drinkers, immortal predators.

Edward could have left.

Instead he moved closer to the window when she typed Cullen.

Nothing useful appeared.

Marie leaned back.

“Vampire,” she whispered.

Edward froze.

Her eyes shifted toward the window.

For one terrible second he thought she had seen him.

She closed the curtains.

Edward stood outside them for another hour.

CHAPTER 9 — SAY IT

The next morning Marie cornered him behind the gym.

“I know what you are.”

Edward's world narrowed to her heartbeat.

“Say it.”

“Vampire.”

He waited for fear.

Marie merely folded her arms.

“Okay,” she said. “That explains the weirdness.”

“The weirdness?”

“The staring. The freezing skin. The never eating. The dramatic driving.”

Edward almost laughed. “You should be frightened.”

“Are you planning to kill me?”

His throat burned.

“No.”

“Then I'm late for class.”

She walked away.

Edward caught her wrist, gently but instantly.

Marie looked down at his hand.

He released her.

“You don't understand what I am.”

“I understand enough.”

“You're not taking this seriously.”

“No, Edward. You're taking me too seriously.”

She left him standing in the rain.

The sentence stayed with him for days.

CHAPTER 10 — THE CULLENS

Alice invited Marie to the Cullen house without asking Edward.

He accused her of interference.

Alice accused him of being unbearable.

Marie arrived because Alice had promised expensive snacks and family gossip.

She liked Esme immediately. Carlisle fascinated her. Emmett made her laugh. Jasper was polite. Rosalie was openly skeptical.

Edward hovered.

Marie noticed.

“Do you need something?”

“No.”

“Then why are you standing three feet away?”

Emmett made a choking sound.

Edward glared at him.

Rosalie's mouth twitched.

Later, Alice showed Marie the house while Edward listened from downstairs.

“He likes you,” Alice said.

Marie shrugged. “That's unfortunate.”

A ceramic ornament cracked in Edward's hand.

Emmett stared at the pieces.

“Oh, man.”

Edward set them down carefully.

Upstairs, Marie continued discussing Alice's wardrobe as if she had not just ruined his evening.

CHAPTER 11 — JEALOUSY

Mike Newton asked Marie to the spring dance.

Edward heard the question from three classrooms away through Mike's mind.

He was beside Marie's locker before Mike finished speaking.

Mike looked at him. “Uh. Cullen?”

Edward smiled without warmth.

Marie looked between them. “Can I help you?”

“No.”

“Then go away.”

Edward did not.

Mike eventually left after Marie said she would think about the dance.

Edward's jaw tightened.

“You shouldn't go with him.”

Marie slowly turned. “Excuse me?”

“He wants—”

“I know what he wants. He asked me.”

“I can hear what he thinks.”

“And I didn't ask you to.”

Edward hated that she was right.

“Are you jealous?” she asked.

“No.”

Marie laughed directly in his face.

Edward decided he hated that even more.

CHAPTER 12 — THE MEADOW

Edward brought Marie to the meadow because he wanted her to understand.

The sun transformed him into something impossible.

Marie stared for a long moment.

“You glitter.”

Edward closed his eyes. “That is not the point.”

“It is a little bit the point.”

He explained hunting. Venom. Immortality. The constant restraint required to sit beside humans who smelled like food.

Then he told her the truth he had avoided.

“Your blood is worse.”

Marie became serious.

“Worse how?”

“Like thirst after years without water.”

“And you sit beside me every day?”

“Yes.”

“That seems stupid.”

“It is.”

“Why?”

Edward looked at her.

Because he could not hear her. Because she dismissed him. Because every scrap of attention from her felt stolen and therefore precious.

“I don't know anymore.”

That was the first completely honest answer he gave her.

CHAPTER 13 — BOUNDARIES

Marie discovered the nighttime visits accidentally.

She woke at 2:08 a.m., opened the curtains, and found Edward standing beneath the tree.

They stared at one another.

Marie opened the window.

“What the hell are you doing?”

Edward could have lied.

“I wanted to know you were safe.”

“At two in the morning?”

“Yes.”

“How often?”

Silence.

“Edward.”

“Most nights.”

Marie looked genuinely angry.

Good. Anger was easier than indifference.

“You don't get to do that.”

“I know.”

“Do you?”

Edward looked toward the forest.

“I know that I should stop.”

“That wasn't my question.”

He looked back at her.

“No,” he admitted. “I don't want to.”

Marie shut the window in his face.

Edward remained outside until sunrise anyway.

CHAPTER 14 — SILENCE AS PUNISHMENT

Marie ignored Edward for six days.

It nearly drove him insane.

No sarcastic remarks in biology. No arguments in the hallway. No annoyed glances across lunch.

Nothing.

She spoke to Alice. She laughed with Emmett. She even tolerated Rosalie.

Edward received silence.

On day six, he caught up to her outside school.

“Please say something.”

Marie kept walking.

“Insult me if you like.”

Nothing.

“Marie.”

She stopped.

“You don't get rewarded with my attention for ignoring my boundaries.”

Edward stared at her.

The sentence struck harder than anger would have.

“What do you want me to do?”

“Stop following me home.”

He hesitated.

Marie raised an eyebrow.

“Yes,” he said finally.

“Good.”

She walked away.

Edward kept the promise.

For exactly nine days.

CHAPTER 15 — THE ACCIDENT

Tyler's van lost control on black ice.

Edward heard the scream before Marie saw the vehicle.

He crossed the parking lot faster than human eyes could track and stopped the van with one hand.

Metal folded around his palm.

Marie stared at the dent.

Then at him.

Edward knew what came next.

Instead she said, “Thank you.”

Two words.

His entire body went still.

She had not thanked him for Port Angeles. Had not thanked him for explanations or warnings or protection she never requested.

This was different.

She would have died.

“You're welcome,” he said quietly.

At the hospital, Carlisle examined her while Edward remained outside the room.

He could hear every thought around her.

Not hers.

Never hers.

And for the first time, the silence frightened him more than it fascinated him.

CHAPTER 16 — LAURENT

Laurent met them in the baseball clearing.

James noticed Marie first.

Victoria noticed Edward noticing James.

The entire world changed in one second.

James inhaled.

Edward stepped in front of Marie.

“No.”

James smiled. “Interesting.”

The Cullens formed a wall.

Marie could feel Edward trembling—not with fear, but violence.

James tilted his head. “She isn't yours.”

Edward's answer came as a growl.

Marie, absurdly, felt annoyed.

“He's right.”

Every vampire looked at her.

Edward turned slowly.

“Marie.”

“What? I'm not property.”

Even Laurent looked amused.

James's smile widened.

Edward looked as though he had discovered a new form of suffering.

CHAPTER 17 — THE HUNT BEGINS

James chose Marie because Edward cared.

That offended her almost as much as being hunted.

The Cullens moved her between cars, houses and false trails. Edward refused to leave her side until Carlisle physically reminded him that James could read the pattern.

“You're making me easier to find,” Marie told him.

“I am not leaving you.”

“Then you're helping him.”

Edward's face went blank.

He hated logic when it came from her.

Alice finally pulled him aside.

“If you want her alive, stop behaving like the world ends six inches from her body.”

Edward looked at Marie across the room.

For him, lately, it did.

He left with Emmett to hunt James.

It was the hardest thing he had done in decades.

CHAPTER 18 — THE STUDIO

James tricked Marie with a recording.

By the time Edward realized, she was already at the ballet studio.

The scent of her blood hit him before he entered.

Everything after that became red.

James died badly.

Edward barely remembered helping kill him.

He remembered Marie on the floor.

He remembered venom spreading from the bite.

He remembered Carlisle telling him to remove it.

Edward pressed his mouth to the wound.

Her blood flooded his senses.

He could have kept drinking.

Some monstrous part of him wanted to.

Then Marie whispered his name—not lovingly, not desperately, simply because he was there.

Edward stopped.

Later he would replay that single syllable hundreds of times.

CHAPTER 19 — RECOVERY

Marie woke in hospital with Edward sitting beside her.

“You look terrible,” she said.

He laughed once, almost broken with relief.

“You nearly died.”

“Yes. I was there.”

Edward leaned forward. “I thought—”

Marie held up a hand.

“No dramatic declarations while I'm medicated.”

He stopped.

She slept again.

Edward remained.

When she woke six hours later, he was in exactly the same position.

“Have you moved?”

“No.”

“That's unsettling.”

“I know.”

Marie closed her eyes again.

Edward smiled.

She was alive enough to be annoyed with him.

It was the most beautiful thing he had ever seen.

CHAPTER 20 — PROM

Marie went to prom because Alice threatened to dress her herself.

Edward appeared in a tuxedo.

Marie stared.

He almost allowed himself hope.

“You look like you're attending your own funeral.”

Hope died quickly.

They danced anyway.

Edward held her carefully.

“You know,” he said, “most people would consider this romantic.”

“Most people aren't dancing with a vampire who used to stand outside their bedroom.”

“Used to?”

Marie narrowed her eyes.

Edward smiled innocently.

She stepped on his shoe.

It did not hurt.

He pretended it did.

CHAPTER 21 — SUMMER

Summer made Edward worse.

School had provided structure. Assigned seats. Bells. Predictable hours in which he could be near Marie without inventing excuses.

Now she had plans that did not involve him.

This was unacceptable to a part of Edward he was increasingly ashamed of.

When Marie spent an afternoon with Angela, he read Angela's thoughts from half a town away whenever possible.

When Marie went hiking with a group, he patrolled the forest without letting himself be seen.

He told himself hidden protection did not violate her boundary if she never knew.

Alice called this “creative dishonesty.”

Rosalie called it “stalking with extra steps.”

Emmett called it “Tuesday.”

Edward ignored them all.

CHAPTER 22 — JACOB BLACK

Jacob Black liked Marie.

Edward knew before Jacob did.

That made the situation intolerable.

At La Push, Edward could not follow. The treaty line held him back while Marie spent an entire afternoon somewhere his gift could not reach.

He paced.

Alice watched from a rock.

“You realize she is allowed to have friends.”

“I know.”

“You look homicidal.”

“I am thinking.”

“You're thinking homicidally.”

When Marie returned, laughing at something Jacob said, Edward's expression hardened.

Marie saw him.

“Oh, absolutely not.”

“I didn't say anything.”

“Your face did.”

Jacob grinned.

Edward disliked him immediately and comprehensively.

CHAPTER 23 — THE WOLF

Jacob's transformation complicated everything.

Marie learned the truth after seeing an enormous russet wolf disappear into the trees and Jacob emerge later wearing half-destroyed clothes.

“Of course,” she muttered. “Why would anything here be normal?”

Edward wanted her away from the wolves.

Marie refused.

“They're dangerous.”

“So are you.”

“That's different.”

“How?”

Edward opened his mouth.

Nothing useful came out.

Marie smiled. “Exactly.”

Jacob heard about the argument and laughed for two days.

CHAPTER 24 — EDWARD LEAVES

Edward decided Marie would be safer without him.

He made the decision alone, which was his first mistake.

He told her in the forest.

“We're leaving Forks.”

Marie stared at him.

He had prepared for tears, anger, disbelief.

She said, “Okay.”

Edward forgot how to speak.

“Okay?”

“You said you're leaving.”

“Yes.”

“So leave.”

He searched her face desperately for pain.

There was some. He thought. Maybe.

But Marie would not give him the scene he had imagined.

“Will you miss me?” he asked before pride could stop him.

Marie looked almost offended by the question.

“You don't get to abandon someone and then ask them to comfort you about it.”

Edward left Forks that night.

Her sentence followed him across three continents.

CHAPTER 25 — WITHOUT HIM

Marie survived.

That was the part Edward had not anticipated.

She was angry. She missed Alice. She missed Emmett. Sometimes, against her better judgment, she missed Edward.

But she lived.

She went to school. Spent time with Jacob. Learned the rhythms of La Push. Laughed again.

Edward, meanwhile, became a ghost.

He tracked rumours. Hunted alone. Called Alice and hung up before she answered.

He had wanted Marie safe.

Instead he had removed himself from her life and discovered that she was perfectly capable of existing without him.

The knowledge was unbearable.

CHAPTER 26 — THE CLIFF

When Alice saw Marie jump from the cliff, the future broke into fragments.

Edward heard the wrong version.

Marie was dead.

For several minutes he ceased to be rational.

He went to Volterra intending to expose himself.

Alice found Marie alive and furious.

“He thinks you're dead.”

Marie stared. “And his solution is to die too?”

“Yes.”

“That is the stupidest thing I've ever heard.”

“Correct.”

Marie went to Italy mostly because she refused to let Edward Cullen turn his own terrible communication skills into an international vampire incident.

CHAPTER 27 — VOLTERRA

Edward stepped into the sunlight.

Marie slammed into him before the crowd saw.

He caught her automatically.

For one perfect second, he thought he was dead already.

Then Marie slapped his shoulder.

“You absolute idiot.”

Edward stared at her.

“You're alive.”

“Obviously.”

He held her tighter.

Marie pushed against him. “Too tight.”

He released her instantly.

The Volturi guards arrived moments later.

Aro found Marie fascinating.

Edward found Aro's fascination unacceptable.

When Aro discovered Edward could not read her mind, his smile widened.

Edward moved closer to Marie.

She moved half a step away just to annoy him.

Even facing possible execution, he noticed.

CHAPTER 28 — THE RETURN

Back in Forks, Edward expected forgiveness.

He did not receive it.

“You don't get to return to exactly where we were,” Marie said.

“I know.”

“Do you?”

Edward hated that question because she always asked it when he did not.

He apologized without excuses.

Marie accepted the apology.

She did not accept him back into every part of her life.

Edward had to earn invitations.

Had to ask before appearing at her house.

Had to tolerate Jacob.

The last requirement nearly killed him.

But he did it.

Mostly.

CHAPTER 29 — THE NOTE

The first note appeared inside Marie's locker.

YOU SMELL LIKE HIS WEAKNESS.

Edward read it and went frighteningly still.

Victoria.

Marie watched his face change.

“No.”

He looked at her. “No what?”

“No locking me in your house. No deciding my schedule. No kidnapping me for my own safety.”

“I had not said—”

“You were thinking it loudly enough.”

For a person whose thoughts Edward could not hear, Marie had become alarmingly good at hearing his.

CHAPTER 30 — THE ARMY

Seattle's murders became impossible to ignore.

Jasper recognized the pattern.

Newborn vampires.

An army.

Edward understood Victoria's plan immediately.

She could not reach Marie through him, so she would drown the Cullens in numbers.

At the family meeting, Edward argued for sending Marie away.

Marie sat beside Rosalie and ate crisps while everybody debated her fate.

Finally she said, “I'm sitting right here.”

Edward stopped.

Rosalie, to everyone's surprise, said, “She's right.”

Marie offered her a crisp.

An alliance was born.

Edward hated it.

CHAPTER 31 — TRAINING

Jasper trained the Cullens and wolves together.

Marie watched from a safe distance.

Edward checked that distance every thirty seconds.

Jacob noticed.

“You know she's not going to evaporate if you look away, right?”

Edward ignored him.

“She might even survive a whole minute.”

Edward turned. “Do you ever stop talking?”

“Not when it annoys you.”

Marie called from the rocks, “I like him.”

Edward's head snapped toward her.

Jacob laughed so hard he nearly missed Jasper's next instruction.

CHAPTER 32 — THE TENT

The mountain was brutally cold.

Marie could not sleep.

Jacob's body heat kept her from freezing, which Edward understood logically and despised emotionally.

Edward sat inches away, perfectly still.

Jacob smirked in the darkness.

“You're grinding your teeth.”

“I don't grind my teeth.”

“You are spiritually grinding your teeth.”

Marie groaned from inside the sleeping bag. “If both of you don't shut up, I'm walking down the mountain myself.”

They became silent immediately.

Ten minutes later Marie fell asleep.

Jacob whispered, “She controls you.”

Edward stared into the darkness.

“No,” he said.

That was precisely why he was obsessed with her.

She didn't.

CHAPTER 33 — VICTORIA

Victoria came for Marie personally.

Edward fought her while Seth held Riley back.

Marie watched the creature that had orchestrated months of terror move like flame through the snow.

Victoria taunted Edward.

“She doesn't love you enough.”

Edward's expression changed.

Victoria had found the wound.

Marie saw it too.

Even now, after everything, Edward did not know what she felt.

He could hear every mind on the mountain except the only one that mattered to him.

Victoria smiled.

Edward killed her anyway.

CHAPTER 34 — AFTER THE FIRE

The remains burned.

Marie sat on a fallen log while Edward crouched in front of her checking injuries she did not have.

“I'm fine.”

“Your wrist.”

“Scratch.”

“Your cheek.”

“Branch.”

“Your—”

“Edward.”

He stopped.

Marie touched his face briefly.

The gesture lasted perhaps two seconds.

Edward became completely still.

“Thank you,” she said.

Then she stood and walked toward the others.

Edward remained crouched beside the fire.

Two seconds.

He would remember them forever.

CHAPTER 35 — THE VOLTURI WATCH

Jane arrived after the battle.

Her red eyes lingered on Marie.

Aro had not forgotten the silent human.

Edward moved between them.

Jane smiled. “Still playing guard dog?”

Marie stepped around Edward.

“He does that. We're working on it.”

Jane looked amused.

Edward did not.

The Volturi left with a warning: Marie knew too much to remain human indefinitely.

That night Edward wanted to discuss transformation.

Marie refused.

“My mortality isn't your emergency.”

Edward stared at the rain outside.

To him, it was.

But he had finally learned not to say every fear as if it were an instruction.

CHAPTER 36 — THE QUESTION

Edward asked Marie one evening in the meadow.

“Do you love me?”

There was no manipulation in his voice this time. No certainty. Just a question he had been afraid to ask.

Marie looked at him for a long time.

“I care about you.”

Edward waited.

“That wasn't what I asked.”

“I know.”

The answer hurt.

He nodded anyway.

Marie leaned back in the grass.

“You're obsessed with outcomes. You want to know what I feel, what I'll choose, whether I'll stay. You want the ending before I've lived the middle.”

Edward looked at her.

“And you?”

“I want you to stop trying to skip ahead.”

For once, Edward listened.

CHAPTER 37 — HUMAN

Marie chose to remain human.

Not forever. Not necessarily.

For now.

Edward struggled with the decision every day.

He imagined car accidents, illness, time, fragile bones, fragile blood vessels, a thousand ordinary ways humans died.

Then he remembered the first lesson Marie had forced upon him:

Protection without consent could become another kind of cage.

So he waited.

He hated waiting.

Marie knew.

That made it slightly more enjoyable.

CHAPTER 38 — THE SECOND FIRST DATE

Their first intentional date happened absurdly late.

No vampire crisis. No hunters. No wolves. No Volturi.

Just dinner in Port Angeles.

Edward actually ordered food and moved it around convincingly.

Marie laughed.

“You've improved.”

“I've had practice.”

“With lying?”

“With pretending to be normal.”

“You're terrible at it.”

Edward smiled.

Outside the restaurant, Marie took his hand first.

He looked down at their fingers.

“Don't make this weird.”

“I wasn't going to.”

“You're staring.”

“I know.”

She did not let go.

CHAPTER 39 — THE LOCKED DOOR

Edward never gained access to Marie's mind.

Years could have passed and he suspected the silence would still fascinate him.

But it changed.

At first, he had treated her mind like a locked room he was entitled to enter.

Now he understood the door belonged to her.

Sometimes she opened it voluntarily.

She told him what frightened her. What annoyed her. What she wanted. What she did not.

Those admissions meant more than stolen thoughts ever could.

One night Marie caught him watching her from the sofa.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“That is obviously a lie.”

Edward smiled.

“I was thinking that I still have no idea what you're thinking.”

Marie smiled back.

“Good.”

He laughed.

For once, the silence did not hurt.

CHAPTER 40 — VEIL

Forks remained grey, wet and full of secrets.

Marie remained human.

Edward remained impossible.

He was still obsessive. He still noticed every change in her heartbeat. Still knew which footsteps were hers from rooms away. Still fought the instinct to follow when she went somewhere alone.

The difference was that now he fought it because she mattered more than the obsession did.

They stood at the edge of the forest after sunset.

“You know,” Marie said, “when I first met you, I thought you were incredibly annoying.”

Edward looked wounded. “First?”

“You're still incredibly annoying.”

“Comforting.”

She started walking.

Edward followed.

Marie looked over her shoulder. “Are you following me again?”

“We are going to the same place.”

“Convenient.”

Edward caught up but did not take her hand.

He waited.

Marie noticed.

After several steps, she reached for him herself.

His fingers closed around hers.

Edward had spent more than a century hearing what people wanted before they said it. Marie had forced him to learn something harder: wanting an answer did not entitle him to it.

He looked at her.

She looked ahead.

“Stop staring.”

“No.”

Marie sighed.

Edward smiled.

Some things, apparently, were incurable.

The rain began again before they reached the road, soft against the trees, drawing a veil across Forks.

Behind them, the forest kept its monsters.

Ahead of them waited whatever Marie chose next.

Edward did not know what that would be.

For the first time, he could live with that.
