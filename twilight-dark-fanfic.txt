VEIL OF FORKS
A Twilight fanfiction
by Elias

A darker alternate-universe fanfiction inspired by Twilight. Bella Swan does not exist in this continuity. Marie arrives in Forks instead.

CHAPTER 1 — THE TOWN THAT WATCHED

Forks looked like the sort of place that had learned to keep secrets by letting the rain erase the evidence.

Marie noticed that before she noticed anything else.

The forest pressed close to the road on both sides, black trunks rising behind curtains of silver rain. Moss swallowed fallen branches. Mist gathered between the trees like breath. Even at midday, the sky seemed undecided about allowing light through.

She rested her forehead against the cold passenger window and watched the town appear in pieces: a petrol station, wet roofs, a diner glowing weakly through the grey, houses tucked beneath enormous trees.

Her new home.

Wonderful.

By Monday morning, Forks High School had already heard about her.

Marie discovered this approximately eleven seconds after stepping out of the car.

People looked.

Not cruelly, mostly. Curiously. A girl with ginger hair bright enough to look almost unnatural beneath the colourless sky was apparently an event in a town where everybody already knew everybody else's childhood injuries.

She ignored most of it.

Then she saw the Cullens.

They sat together at lunch as though an invisible line had been drawn around their table.

Beautiful was too simple a word. Beautiful implied warmth. They looked sculpted rather than born—pale, motionless, too symmetrical. Rosalie Hale was breathtaking in a way that bordered on hostile. Emmett Cullen looked like he could pick up the cafeteria and move it six feet to the left if somebody asked. Alice Cullen was tiny and watchful. Jasper Hale sat rigidly beside her.

And Edward Cullen was staring directly at Marie.

Not admiring her.

Not curious.

Horrified.

Marie stopped with her drink halfway to her mouth.

His fingers had curled around the edge of the table hard enough that the tendons stood out beneath his skin.

Then he was gone.

His chair scraped backward.

He crossed the cafeteria too quickly.

Nobody else seemed to notice.

Marie did.

She watched the doors swing shut behind him.

"Well," she muttered. "Nice meeting you too."

Across the room, Alice Cullen smiled.

Not at the joke.

At Marie.

As if she knew something Marie didn't.

That evening, Edward Cullen stood alone among the trees behind Marie's house.

He had told himself he would only make certain she was safe.

That was the first lie.

The second was that he could leave whenever he wanted.

Through the rain-darkened window he could see Marie moving around her bedroom, unaware of the pale figure hidden beyond the tree line.

He could hear her heartbeat.

Steady.

Alive.

Impossible.

Her scent had nearly destroyed him in biology.

But that wasn't why he had followed her.

Edward had heard thousands upon thousands of human thoughts during his existence. Petty ones. Tender ones. Violent ones. Boring ones. Minds had always surrounded him like a room full of radios that could never be switched off.

Marie was silence.

Complete silence.

He should have found it peaceful.

Instead, it frightened him.

Because for the first time in nearly a century, Edward Cullen could look at a human being and have absolutely no idea what she might do next.

Behind the glass, Marie suddenly stopped moving.

Edward froze.

Slowly, she turned toward the window.

For one impossible second, her eyes seemed to settle directly on the place where he stood.

He knew she couldn't see him.

Still, something cold moved through him.

Marie walked closer to the window.

Edward disappeared before she reached it.

She pulled the curtain aside.

Nothing stood beneath the trees.

Only rain.

Only darkness.

And two footprints in the mud that stopped directly beneath her window.

CHAPTER 2 — THE EMPTY SEAT

Edward Cullen did not return to school the next day.

Or the day after that.

Marie pretended not to care.

By Friday, pretending had become irritating.

His empty chair in biology sat beside hers like an accusation. Mr. Banner continued talking about cellular respiration while Marie occasionally glanced at the vacant seat and wondered what exactly she had done to make a stranger flee the building.

On Monday, he returned.

He was already seated when she entered.

Marie stopped beside the table.

Edward looked up.

Whatever expression she had expected—disgust, irritation, another glimpse of that strange panic—was gone.

He looked controlled.

Too controlled.

"Hello," he said.

Marie put her bag down. "Oh, you speak."

Something flickered at the corner of his mouth.

"Apparently."

"I wasn't sure. You disappeared pretty dramatically last week."

"I was ill."

Marie looked at his flawless pale face.

"You look terminal."

This time he laughed.

It startled her.

It seemed to startle him too.

For the rest of class Edward behaved almost normally, if normal included never fidgeting, never appearing to breathe unless somebody was watching, and answering questions before Mr. Banner finished asking them.

Marie noticed everything.

Edward noticed that she noticed.

That was the problem.

He had spent the weekend hunting far north with Carlisle and Emmett, putting as much distance as possible between himself and the girl whose blood had burned through his throat like venom.

It had helped.

Barely.

But distance had created another hunger.

Curiosity.

Who was she?

Why couldn't he hear her?

Why had Alice seen flashes of Marie standing beside them in futures that should have been impossible?

And why, in several of those futures, was Edward covered in blood?

Not Marie's.

Someone else's.

At the end of class, Marie reached for her bag.

Edward spoke before he could stop himself.

"You shouldn't walk home alone."

She looked at him.

"Excuse me?"

He had heard it that morning from the mind of a boy two tables away. A cougar had been seen near the eastern trails. Human concern would have been reasonable.

Edward's concern was not human.

"There have been animal sightings near town," he said.

"So?"

"So be careful."

Marie studied him long enough to make even a vampire uncomfortable.

"Do you always give ominous warnings to people you barely know?"

"Only the ones who look unlikely to listen."

"Then you're wasting your time."

She walked away.

Edward watched her leave.

Alice appeared beside him a moment later.

"That went well," she said brightly.

"Go away, Alice."

"You know, in one version she throws a book at you."

Edward turned.

"Why?"

Alice grinned.

"Haven't seen that part yet."

That afternoon Marie walked home alone.

Naturally.

The rain had weakened to a mist. She took the longer road because she wanted to see the edge of the forest again.

Halfway home, the woods went silent.

No birds.

No dripping branches.

Nothing.

Marie slowed.

There was something on the road behind her.

A soft scrape.

She turned.

Empty asphalt.

Then movement flashed between the trees.

Too large for a dog.

Marie backed away.

A low growl rolled through the forest.

Her body understood the sound before her mind did.

Run.

She took one step.

Something enormous burst from the undergrowth.

Marie barely saw tawny fur and teeth before another shape struck it sideways.

The impact sounded like a car crash.

The animal flew into a tree.

Marie stumbled backward.

Edward Cullen stood between her and the cougar.

He wasn't breathing.

His eyes were black.

And his lips had pulled back from teeth that suddenly looked far too sharp.

The cougar fled.

Edward didn't chase it.

He turned toward Marie.

For several seconds neither of them spoke.

Rain gathered in his bronze hair.

Marie stared at the tree the cougar had hit.

The trunk was cracked.

Then she looked at Edward.

"You," she said slowly, "are going to explain that."

CHAPTER 3 — BEAUTIFUL MONSTERS

Edward tried lying.

Marie let him finish.

Then she said, "That was terrible. Try again."

They stood beneath the cover of an abandoned bus shelter while rain rattled against the roof.

"Adrenaline," Edward said.

"You threw a cougar into a tree."

"It lunged."

"Yes. Cougars famously become weightless while lunging."

Edward stared at her.

Marie folded her arms.

"Also, you crossed about twenty metres before I could blink."

Silence.

"And," she continued, "you don't breathe properly."

That caught him.

"I breathe."

"When you remember."

Edward's expression changed.

For the first time since Marie had met him, the performance dropped entirely.

He looked old.

Not physically. Something behind his face did.

"You should be afraid of me," he said.

Marie considered him.

"Are you going to hurt me?"

His jaw tightened.

"I don't know."

It was not the answer she expected.

Oddly, it was the answer she trusted.

Edward stepped closer.

"Your blood is... difficult for me."

Marie raised an eyebrow. "That sentence did not improve the situation."

A humourless smile touched his face.

"No. I suppose not."

The truth arrived in pieces.

Cold skin.

Impossible speed.

Strength.

No sleep.

No aging.

Venom.

Blood.

Marie listened until there was only one sensible word left.

"Vampire."

Edward nodded once.

The forest beyond the shelter seemed darker afterward.

"And your family?"

"The same."

"All of them?"

"Yes."

Marie thought of Alice's strange smile.

"That explains a lot."

Edward actually looked offended.

"That's your reaction?"

"I'm processing."

"Most humans would run."

"Most humans didn't just watch you save them."

His expression hardened.

"Don't mistake restraint for goodness."

There it was again.

That darkness in him.

Marie understood suddenly that Edward Cullen did not fear that she would discover he was a monster.

He feared she would discover it and stay anyway.

"Why can't you hear me?" she asked.

Edward became very still.

Marie smiled faintly.

"Alice told me nothing. You're just incredibly obvious whenever something bothers you."

He looked away.

"I hear thoughts."

"Everyone's?"

"Everyone's except yours."

Marie blinked.

That explained the staring.

"So you've been trying to figure me out because I'm the one person whose privacy you can't invade?"

"When you say it like that—"

"How else should I say it?"

Edward laughed under his breath.

Then the sound stopped.

His eyes moved toward the forest.

"We need to go."

"Why?"

"Because we're not alone."

Far between the trees, something pale moved.

Not an animal.

A person.

Edward stepped in front of Marie.

The stranger emerged slowly from the woods.

A man with red eyes.

He smiled.

"Well," he said, looking directly at Marie. "That smells interesting."

Edward's entire body changed.

He crouched slightly.

A snarl tore from his chest so violently that Marie felt it through the soles of her shoes.

The stranger's smile widened.

"Easy, Cullen."

Edward did not move.

"Leave."

"I was only curious."

"Be curious somewhere else."

The stranger looked around Edward at Marie.

And Marie understood something instantly.

Edward had been right.

There were monsters in Forks.

She simply hadn't realized yet that Edward might not be the one she needed to fear most.

CHAPTER 4 — THE CULLEN HOUSE

Carlisle Cullen was not what Marie expected the patriarch of a vampire family to look like.

For one thing, he was wearing a cardigan.

For another, he apologized.

"Edward should have brought you here sooner," Carlisle said gently.

Edward stood across the living room looking as though he would rather be struck by lightning.

"She wasn't supposed to know."

"And yet," Rosalie said from the staircase, "here she is."

Marie looked up.

Rosalie's beauty was somehow worse at close range.

She also looked furious.

"Hello to you too."

Emmett coughed into his fist to hide a laugh.

Alice didn't bother hiding hers.

The Cullen house was bright, open, and almost aggressively unlike a vampire lair. No coffins. No candles dripping blood. Mostly glass, wood, expensive furniture, and a piano Edward seemed determined not to acknowledge.

Esme welcomed Marie warmly.

Jasper kept his distance.

Alice hugged her after knowing her for approximately four minutes.

Rosalie watched the entire thing as though Marie were an unexploded bomb somebody had placed on the carpet.

Later, Alice found Marie alone near the windows.

"Don't mind Rosalie."

"She hates me."

"She hates situations she can't control."

Marie glanced toward Edward.

"Runs in the family?"

Alice's grin was immediate.

"Oh, I like you."

"You already knew you would."

Alice's smile faded just slightly.

Marie caught it.

"What?"

"My gift isn't like Edward's," Alice said. "I see possibilities. Futures based on decisions."

"And you've seen me."

"A lot."

Something in Alice's tone chilled her.

"Good futures?"

Alice looked toward Edward.

"Some."

"And the others?"

Alice was silent.

That evening Edward drove Marie home.

Neither spoke for several minutes.

"The red-eyed vampire," Marie finally said. "Who was he?"

"A nomad."

"Name?"

"Laurent."

"And he wanted to kill me."

Edward's hands tightened on the steering wheel.

"He was interested."

"That's a very diplomatic way to describe somebody smelling me like dinner."

"He won't touch you."

Marie turned toward him.

"You can't promise that."

Edward looked at her.

His face was beautiful in the dim light from the dashboard.

And terrifying.

"Yes," he said quietly. "I can."

Marie should have found the certainty comforting.

Instead she wondered what Edward Cullen would be willing to do to keep that promise.

She got her answer sooner than expected.

At three seventeen in the morning, Edward stood over a body in the forest.

The man was not dead.

Not yet.

He was human—a hunter who had seen too much near the Cullen property and decided photographs of impossible pale figures might be worth money.

Edward held the man's phone in one hand.

Every image had been deleted.

The hunter trembled on the ground.

"Please."

Edward stared down at him.

He could hear the man's thoughts.

Fear.

Confusion.

Plans to return.

Plans to tell others.

Plans involving Marie's description.

Edward's expression became empty.

"You saw nothing," he said.

The man nodded desperately.

Edward listened to his thoughts.

He was lying.

A hand settled on Edward's shoulder.

Carlisle.

"Enough."

For a moment Edward did not move.

Then he stepped away.

Carlisle helped the hunter stand.

Edward looked toward the distant lights of Forks.

Toward Marie.

The frightening part wasn't that he had nearly killed a man.

The frightening part was how little he regretted it.

CHAPTER 5 — THE THING IN THE TREES

Marie began dreaming about the forest.

In every dream, something followed her.

Sometimes it had red eyes.

Sometimes golden ones.

Sometimes she never saw its face at all.

Then people started disappearing.

A hiker first.

Then a fisherman.

Forks treated disappearance differently than cities did. There were no screaming headlines. Just lowered voices in diners, search parties in fluorescent jackets, and worried glances toward the trees.

Edward became unbearable.

"You're not going anywhere alone."

Marie looked up from her locker.

"Good morning to you too."

"I'm serious."

"That's usually the problem."

He stepped closer.

"Something is hunting near town."

"Laurent?"

"No."

That was worse.

"Then who?"

"I don't know."

For Edward Cullen, those words sounded almost obscene.

The answer came two nights later.

Marie woke to tapping against her bedroom window.

Three slow knocks.

Tap.

Tap.

Tap.

She sat upright.

Rain streaked the glass.

Nothing outside.

Then lightning flashed.

A woman stood beyond the window.

Barefoot.

Red-haired.

Smiling.

Marie couldn't breathe.

The woman lifted one finger to her lips.

Then vanished.

The window exploded inward.

Marie screamed and rolled from the bed as glass scattered across the floor.

A pale hand caught the window frame.

Before the woman could enter, something hit her from the side.

Edward.

They disappeared into the darkness.

The sounds from the forest were inhuman.

Trees cracking.

Snarls.

Stone striking stone.

Marie ran downstairs.

Alice was already inside the house.

"Shoes," Alice ordered.

"What?"

"Put shoes on. We're leaving."

"Edward—"

"Is buying us time."

"Who is she?"

Alice's face went grim.

"Victoria."

The name meant nothing.

Yet.

Alice grabbed Marie's hand.

"And she isn't here because she's hungry."

Outside, another tree broke.

"Then why?"

Alice looked at her.

"Because somebody she loved died."

Marie understood.

"A Cullen killed him."

"Yes."

"Edward?"

Alice hesitated.

That was answer enough.

Marie looked toward the forest.

Somewhere in the darkness Edward was fighting something that had come to kill her because of him.

And for the first time, she wondered whether meeting Edward Cullen had saved her life.

Or shortened it.

CHAPTER 6 — NO SAFE DISTANCE

They took Marie to the Cullen house.

Edward returned just before dawn.

His shirt was torn. A long crack ran across his shoulder like fractured marble, already knitting itself closed.

Marie stood the second she saw him.

"Did you kill her?"

"No."

"Then she'll come back."

Edward said nothing.

"Won't she?"

"Yes."

Marie laughed once, without humour.

"Fantastic."

Edward approached carefully.

"Marie—"

"Don't."

He stopped.

"You knew there were others watching me."

"I suspected."

"You followed me home before I even knew what you were."

Edward's expression sharpened.

Marie saw the truth immediately.

"You did."

"I was protecting you."

"I hadn't asked you to."

"You didn't know you needed protection."

"That doesn't make it less insane."

The room went silent.

Emmett suddenly found the ceiling fascinating.

Alice quietly pulled Jasper toward another room.

Edward's voice dropped.

"Do you want me to leave you alone?"

Marie opened her mouth.

Nothing came out.

That angered her more.

Because she should have said yes.

Edward was dangerous. Obsessive. Secretive. His world had put a target on her bedroom window.

But the thought of him actually disappearing created an unexpected hollow feeling beneath her ribs.

Edward watched her face, unable to hear the answer forming in her mind.

"I don't know," she said.

For once, he didn't push.

"Then I'll wait until you do."

He kept his distance for three days.

On the fourth, Marie found him sitting outside her house in the rain.

"This is your version of distance?"

Edward looked up.

"I'm across the street."

"How generous."

"Victoria came within six miles last night."

Marie sat beside him on the wet hood of his car.

"You could have told me."

"You wanted space."

"Space and information can coexist."

"I'm learning."

Marie looked at him.

"Slowly."

"Painfully."

For a while they listened to the rain.

Then Edward said, "There's something else."

Marie groaned. "Of course there is."

"The Volturi know about you."

"Who are the Volturi?"

Edward's expression answered before his words did.

"The reason vampires have rules."

CHAPTER 7 — RED EYES IN ITALY

The letter arrived without postage.

Cream paper.

Black wax.

One sentence.

Bring the silent girl to Volterra.

Marie read it twice.

"Absolutely not."

Carlisle looked troubled.

Edward looked murderous.

Alice looked somewhere else entirely, watching futures unfold behind her eyes.

"If we ignore them," Carlisle said, "they'll come here."

"Then let them," Edward said.

"And risk Forks?"

Edward went silent.

Marie hated that everybody was discussing her like a diplomatic package with hair.

"I'll go."

Edward turned instantly.

"No."

"I wasn't asking."

"Marie."

"If they come here, people die."

"If you go there, you might die."

"Then come with me."

That ended the argument.

Volterra was beautiful in the way poisonous flowers were beautiful.

Ancient stone. Warm sunlight. Bells echoing across narrow streets.

Beneath it all lived something old enough to make the Cullens seem young.

Aro received Marie like an honoured guest.

That frightened her more than hostility would have.

"Extraordinary," he whispered.

His red eyes moved over her face.

Edward stood so close beside Marie that their shoulders touched.

"May I?" Aro asked, extending his hand.

Edward's body tensed.

Marie looked at Aro's hand.

"What happens if I say no?"

Aro smiled.

"How refreshing."

He withdrew it.

Jane did not smile.

She stared at Marie.

Something invisible struck.

Nothing happened.

Jane blinked.

Edward looked at Marie.

Aro's delight became terrifying.

"Not merely silent," he murmured. "Shielded."

Marie suddenly understood why the Volturi wanted her.

Not because she knew vampires existed.

Because she was something they couldn't control.

Aro offered immortality before they left.

"Think of it," he said. "No mind may enter yours. Perhaps no gift may touch you. Eternity could make you magnificent."

Marie met his red eyes.

"I'm already difficult enough."

Aro laughed.

Edward didn't.

Because Edward had heard the thought behind Aro's smile.

If Marie would not join the Volturi willingly, one day they would find another way.

CHAPTER 8 — THE PRICE OF STAYING

Back in Forks, nothing felt ordinary anymore.

Marie knew monsters existed beneath cities.

She knew some wore doctor’s coats and refused human blood.

Others wore ancient robes and collected gifted vampires like jewels.

And one sat beside her in biology pretending to care about mitosis.

"You're staring," Marie whispered.

"I do that."

"I've noticed."

"You almost died in Italy."

"Technically, I almost died several times before Italy."

Edward's pencil snapped.

Marie looked at it.

"Very normal response."

After school they drove into the forest.

Edward showed her the meadow he went to when the world became too loud.

There, beneath rare sunlight, his skin caught the light like fractured crystal.

Marie stared.

Edward looked almost embarrassed.

"This is why?"

"Partly."

"You hide from humanity because you're sparkly?"

"Marie."

She laughed.

He tried not to.

Failed.

For a moment they were simply two people sitting in a field.

Then Edward's smile faded.

"I can't give you a normal life."

"I didn't ask you for one."

"You should."

"Stop deciding what I should want."

He looked down.

Marie reached for his hand.

Cold fingers closed carefully around hers.

"I'm not promising forever," she said.

Edward's eyes lifted.

"I'm promising today."

For someone immortal, today should have meant nothing.

To Edward, it meant everything.

He leaned closer, slowly enough for Marie to stop him.

She didn't.

Their first kiss was careful.

Too careful.

Until Marie caught the front of his shirt and kissed him again.

Edward made a sound somewhere between surprise and surrender.

When they separated, his forehead rested against hers.

"You are catastrophically bad for my self-control," he murmured.

"Sounds personal."

"It is extremely personal."

Neither noticed the red-haired figure watching from the distant tree line.

CHAPTER 9 — THE HUNT

Victoria stopped running.

She started planning.

The first newborn appeared outside Seattle.

Then another.

Then twelve.

Carlisle understood before anyone said it aloud.

"An army."

Marie felt Edward's hand close around hers.

"For me?"

Jasper nodded.

He had seen newborn armies before.

He knew what they could do.

Forks became a battlefield nobody human could know existed.

The Cullens prepared.

The wolves came too.

That was how Marie discovered Forks had been keeping more than one supernatural secret.

Jacob Black looked at Edward like murder was a hobby he was considering taking up.

Edward returned the expression.

Marie stood between them.

"If either of you starts measuring territory by peeing on trees, I'm leaving."

Jacob choked.

Emmett laughed for almost a full minute.

The battle began before sunrise.

Marie was hidden high in the mountains with Edward and Seth while the others fought below.

That had been the plan.

Victoria had planned better.

She appeared from the trees without warning.

Red hair.

Red eyes.

A smile Marie remembered from her bedroom window.

Edward moved in front of her.

Victoria circled.

"You took everything from me," she said.

Edward's voice was cold.

"He chose his fate."

"Then she chooses yours."

Victoria attacked.

The fight was too fast for Marie's eyes.

Snow exploded beneath their feet. Stone cracked. Seth lunged at another vampire emerging from the ridge.

Edward and Victoria vanished between trees and reappeared seconds later locked together.

Then Victoria broke away.

Toward Marie.

Edward shouted.

Marie had one second.

She grabbed the jagged stone beside her and sliced her palm.

Blood filled the frozen air.

Every vampire stopped.

Victoria turned instinctively toward the scent.

So did Edward.

For one horrifying heartbeat, his black eyes fixed on Marie's bleeding hand.

Victoria smiled.

Wrong choice.

Edward moved.

When it ended, the mountain was silent.

Victoria was gone.

Edward stood several metres away from Marie, shaking.

Not from the fight.

From her blood.

"Don't come closer," he whispered.

Marie wrapped her hand in torn fabric.

"Edward."

"Please."

His face looked tortured.

Marie understood then what loving him actually meant.

Not pretending he wasn't dangerous.

Not believing love magically made the monster disappear.

It meant seeing the monster clearly.

And trusting the person fighting it.

She stayed where she was.

"I'm not going anywhere."

Edward closed his eyes.

CHAPTER 10 — VEIL

Winter settled over Forks.

The missing-person posters disappeared one by one.

Humans forgot.

Humans were good at forgetting things that frightened them when given a reasonable explanation.

Marie wasn't.

She remembered Volterra.

Victoria.

The newborns.

Edward standing beneath her bedroom window before she even knew his name.

Some things between them became easier.

Others became stranger.

Edward still occasionally appeared outside her house without warning.

Marie started opening the window and throwing socks at him.

Alice kept seeing futures she refused to fully explain.

Rosalie stopped openly disliking Marie, which apparently counted as affection.

Emmett began taking bets on how long Edward could go without being overprotective.

His record was eleven minutes.

Then Alice saw something that changed everything.

She was sitting beside Marie at the Cullen house when her body suddenly went rigid.

The glass in her hand slipped.

Edward caught it before it hit the floor.

"Alice?"

Her eyes focused slowly.

She looked at Marie.

Not Edward.

Marie felt cold.

"What did you see?"

Alice swallowed.

"You."

"Doing what?"

Alice looked toward the windows.

Outside, snow drifted silently through the forest.

"Standing in Volterra."

Edward went completely still.

"When?"

"I don't know."

"Human?" Marie asked.

Alice hesitated.

Edward turned toward her.

"Alice."

Alice's voice was barely audible.

"No."

Silence swallowed the room.

Marie looked down at her hands.

Warm.

Living.

Temporary.

Edward knelt in front of her.

"A vision isn't destiny."

Marie looked into his golden eyes.

"I know."

But that night, lying awake while rain whispered against the windows, Marie thought about immortality.

Not the romantic version.

The real one.

Thirst.

Blood.

Watching humans age.

Watching centuries pass.

Becoming something that could never entirely belong among the living again.

Outside, a familiar figure stood beneath the trees.

Marie opened the window.

"You're being creepy again."

Edward looked up.

"You were awake."

"Not an excuse."

A moment later he was sitting on the window ledge.

Marie made room.

Neither spoke about Alice's vision.

Not yet.

Edward took her hand.

His skin was cold.

Hers wasn't.

Beyond them, Forks disappeared beneath rain and darkness.

The town still had secrets.

The forest still had teeth.

And somewhere thousands of miles away, an ancient vampire king was waiting patiently for the silent girl to become something more.

Marie leaned against Edward's shoulder.

"Do you think we're making a terrible decision?"

Edward looked at their joined hands.

"Almost certainly."

Marie smiled.

"Good."

His mouth curved.

"I was worried you might start behaving sensibly."

"Never."

Edward kissed her hair.

Outside, something moved between the trees.

Neither of them saw it.

Not this time.

EPILOGUE — ARO'S LETTER

My dear Marie,

I have always believed patience to be the most civilized form of hunger.

You declined my invitation once.

I will not insult either of us by pretending that means you will decline forever.

Immortality has a curious way of finding those who stand too close to it.

When the day arrives—and I believe it will—remember that Volterra keeps its doors open for extraordinary things.

Until then, enjoy your heartbeat.

They are so terribly brief.

— Aro
