The Door Behind the Radiator

by Marie Dudek

Chapter 1 – Gray Winter
Saint Petersburg in February had a way of swallowing light. The sun never truly rose; it only smeared a dull silver glow across the sky, as though someone had rubbed the day with an eraser and left a smudge behind.
Fifteen-year-old Michael Petrov trudged home from school through slush that clung to his boots like glue. His backpack was heavy with textbooks, but heavier still was the silence waiting for him in Apartment 47.
The building was old—one of those Soviet blocks with stairwells that smelled of rust and wet wool. Each step creaked as though remembering better days. Michael passed the neighbor’s door, where a string of garlic hung for luck, then his own. The key stuck in the lock, as always, before giving way with a sigh.
Inside: warmth, but not the comforting kind. The radiators hissed and popped, metal sighing under pressure. His mother’s coat was gone from the hook—she was at her second shift again. A note waited on the kitchen table, written in her tired slanted hand:
Soup in the pot. Do homework. Love you. — M.
Michael smiled faintly. He heated the soup and watched the window fog as snow drifted outside. Somewhere below, a dog barked. Somewhere above, a baby cried. Ordinary sounds, but tonight they all felt far away.
He tried to read his literature assignment—Pushkin’s The Queen of Spades—but the radiator kept humming. It wasn’t the usual metal groan of hot water. This was softer, steadier… a low whispering tone that rose and fell, almost as if the pipes were breathing.
Michael tilted his head, listening.
There. Beneath the hiss—something almost like words.
He shook it off, muttering, “Just the pipes.” He’d heard stories about them—old buildings speaking through their bones.
But later, when he lay in bed, the humming came again. Only now it was under his pillow, pulsing through the floor.
And somewhere between waking and sleep, he thought he heard it clearly:
“Mikhail… come closer.”











Chapter 2 – The Whispers
Morning came reluctantly, pale and tired. Michael’s alarm buzzed, but he had already been awake for an hour, watching the ceiling. The hum from the radiator had faded sometime before dawn, leaving behind a silence that felt too deep, too aware.
He got dressed, ate his breakfast of cold kasha, and told himself it had been a dream. Buildings made sounds. Pipes whispered. It didn’t mean anything.
But as he pulled on his boots and stepped into the corridor, he noticed frost crawling up the wall behind the radiator—tiny white veins spreading in the shape of a spiral. He touched it; it melted instantly, leaving his fingertips damp.

School passed in a gray blur. He took notes he didn’t read, stared out the window at the falling snow. When the final bell rang, he lingered, waiting for the classrooms to empty. The corridors were quieter in winter, everyone rushing home to warmth.
Michael liked the emptiness. It was honest.
He made it home before his mother again. The apartment smelled faintly of metal and boiled potatoes. He hung his coat, tossed his bag aside, and stood in the hallway listening.
The radiator waited. Silent. Still.
He almost felt disappointed.

Later that evening, as the light died behind the curtains, the sound returned—soft at first, almost kind. The radiator exhaled, the pipes shivered, and then a hum began: low, even, rhythmic.
Michael sat cross-legged on the carpet, pencil poised above his math homework. He tried to focus. But the hum grew louder, almost melodic.
And then, beneath it, something else—a murmur, faint as breath.
He froze.
It wasn’t words exactly, not at first. Just shapes of sound, fragments. But as he leaned closer, his ear almost touching the hot metal, the noise sharpened.
“…come… closer…”
He jerked back, heart hammering. The voice—or whatever it was—faded into the pipes again.
“Okay,” he whispered to no one. “That’s new.”
He waited. Ten seconds. Thirty. The hum steadied, patient.
He picked up the pencil, pretended to work. But curiosity gnawed at him. He leaned in again, this time pressing his palm to the warm metal.
The whisper returned.
“…not afraid… are you?”
Michael snatched his hand away. The radiator clanged in protest, a sharp metallic ping that echoed down the hall.
He sat perfectly still, barely breathing.
From behind the radiator came a soft ticking sound, like something small tapping against the wall. Once. Twice. Three times. Then silence again.
When his mother came home an hour later, Michael didn’t mention it. She was tired—her voice worn, her eyes shadowed. He helped her set the table, listened to her stories about the hospital, and laughed where he was supposed to.
But all through dinner, he could feel it—the faint vibration in the floorboards, the steady pulse from the wall.
As if something on the other side was breathing with him.

That night he dreamed of snow falling inside the apartment, quiet and endless, covering the furniture in white. When he woke, there was frost on his windowsill—and a tiny patch of peeled paint behind the radiator, shaped like the edge of a door.






















Chapter 3 – The Little Door
The next day crept by like a long breath held too long. Michael couldn’t stop thinking about that patch of peeled paint. It had looked almost deliberate — a small square corner, just enough to notice, just enough to wonder.
By the time he came home from school, the winter sky was already dimming into violet. His mother had left another note on the table:
Late shift again. There’s bread and stew. Love you.
The words used to comfort him. Now they only reminded him how empty the apartment felt.
He set his bag down quietly and stood in the hallway, facing the radiator. The air around it was warmer than the rest of the apartment, pulsing faintly. The hum had returned — soft, steady, like a lullaby hummed through metal.
He crouched.
The paint on the wall behind the radiator was cracked in a neat square. The corners lifted slightly, just enough to slip a fingernail under. He tried, but the plaster flaked and crumbled.
Michael frowned. He went to the kitchen, found a butter knife in the drawer, and returned. The blade scraped against the wall with a small hiss. Flakes of white paint fell like dry snow.
The hum deepened.
He hesitated — half expecting his mother’s voice to tell him to stop — but the apartment stayed silent. So he kept going. The line widened, revealing the edge of a small metal door no bigger than a shoebox, the kind that didn’t belong in any ordinary wall.
His heart quickened. He brushed away the remaining paint, revealing a tiny rusted latch.
“Old maintenance panel,” he whispered to himself. But even as he said it, he didn’t believe it.
He wrapped his fingers around the latch and pulled. It resisted. Then, with a soft metallic sigh, it swung open.
Behind it — darkness.
Not the normal, flat kind of dark that hides dust and wiring. This darkness looked thick, layered, like smoke folded over itself. A cold breath drifted out and kissed his face. It smelled faintly of pine and something sweet — winter air trapped in an old dream.
Michael leaned closer, shining his phone flashlight into the gap. The beam revealed a narrow tunnel, bricks glistening as though damp. The space extended farther than it should — much farther than the wall’s thickness allowed.
His breath clouded in front of him. He reached in with one hand, half expecting to touch a back panel. But his fingers met only cold air.
And then, faintly, from somewhere down the tunnel, a voice — barely more than a sigh:
“Michael…”
He stumbled back, nearly dropping the phone. The beam swung wildly across the hallway, scattering shadows.
The voice had been soft. Familiar, almost tender.
“Mama?” he whispered.
Silence. Only the radiator’s hum.
He stared at the little door, heart pounding. The sensible part of him said to close it, to forget it existed. But the air seeping from that tunnel was cool and alive, carrying the scent of snow and trees — things he hadn’t felt in months.
He glanced toward the front door, half-expecting his mother to appear. She didn’t. The world outside this apartment might as well have stopped existing.
Michael took a deep breath.
And then, on hands and knees, he crawled inside.

The tunnel was smaller than it had looked — tight and uneven. The bricks were slick under his palms, and the air hummed faintly with that same soft rhythm, as though the world itself were breathing. His flashlight flickered, its glow trembling against the walls.
He moved forward. One meter. Two.
Then the tunnel began to widen. The darkness ahead shimmered faintly, not like shadow but like light seen through water.
Michael reached out and touched it.
Cold, smooth, endless.
And then, all at once, the hum became a voice again — clearer now, warm and full:
“You’ve found me, my boy.”
The air shifted. The tunnel opened like a mouth, and Michael crawled out onto a soft carpet.
He blinked.
He was in his apartment. Or… something that looked like it.
The wallpaper wasn’t cracked. The lights glowed a steady gold. The radiator shone like new. And from the kitchen came the smell of fresh soup — thick, savory, perfect.
“Michael,” said a voice behind him.
He turned.
His mother stood there, smiling.
Only her eyes were black and glassy as stone.






Chapter 4 – The Familiar and the Strange
Michael blinked. The soup smelled perfect. Steam curled into the golden light. He could hear the radiator singing softly, but it wasn’t the same rhythm as the one in his real apartment. This one felt… alive, watching.
He looked around. The apartment was like his own, yet everything was slightly different. The wallpaper’s floral pattern was brighter, fresher, almost glowing. The furniture sat straighter, the edges sharper, like someone had pressed it into memory rather than letting it exist.
And then he saw her.
His mother.
She smiled at him. Warm, gentle, familiar. But her eyes… they were black glass, reflecting the light of the room yet showing nothing of her soul.
“Michael,” she said, voice soft and smooth. “You’ve come at last.”
His stomach clenched. The voice was his mother’s, but… not hers.
“I… I don’t understand,” he stammered. “Where am I?”
“You’re home,” she said simply, moving toward him. “I’ve been waiting.”
Michael swallowed. Something about the air felt thick, almost sweet, like honey mixed with snow. He wanted to step back but couldn’t stop looking. Every feature of this apartment, this woman, felt like a memory folded over itself — perfect, polished, impossible.
She set a bowl of soup in front of him. Steam rose, carrying the scent of dill and potatoes, exactly like his favorite recipe. He wanted to touch it, to taste it, but a shiver ran down his spine.
“Eat, Michael,” she said. “Everything you want… everything you need… it’s here.”
He glanced around. Shadows moved strangely. Where the corners should have been dark, the light stretched, bending softly, as if the room itself were breathing.
“I… I should go back,” he said quietly.
She tilted her head, smiling wider. “Why would you go back, my boy? Here is warmer. Here is safe. You don’t have to be lonely anymore.”
Michael’s heart pounded. He wanted to run, but his legs felt like lead. The air hummed around him, a quiet rhythm he could almost step to, almost fall into. It whispered his name, soft, patient, insistent:
“Michael… stay…”
He tried to shake the feeling, telling himself it was imagination. His real apartment was somewhere beyond this shimmering wall of light and shadow. He was dreaming. This wasn’t real.
And yet…
He took a step toward the hallway, and the floor seemed to stretch beneath him, soft and pliant, as if it had a pulse. His mother—or the thing that looked like her—moved with him, matching every step.
“Michael,” she whispered, “you could have everything here. Just stay. You don’t have to struggle. You don’t have to feel alone.”
Something in her voice twisted, a thread of impatience, sharp as frost. He froze. For a moment, her smile faltered, like a mask slipping.
The room shifted. The walls seemed taller, the ceiling higher. A shadow stretched across the floor, long and thin, not matching her figure. It pulsed faintly, like it had a heartbeat of its own.
Michael swallowed hard. He wanted to run, but he didn’t know where to go. Every doorway led only to more of the same — the same perfect apartment, the same impossibly clean kitchen, the same black-eyed woman smiling at him.
He realized then that this world wasn’t real. It was a copy. A dream. But it had noticed him. It was alive. And he had stepped into it.
The soup in front of him steamed, inviting. His mother—or the version of her—beckoned him closer.
Michael shook his head. He couldn’t stay.
But the pull was strong. Stronger than hunger, stronger than fear. A whisper ran along the walls:
“You are mine…”
Michael swallowed, heart racing, knowing that whatever this place was, it didn’t want him to leave.
And yet he had to. Somehow.
















Chapter 5 – The World That Loves You
Michael sat frozen, staring at the steaming bowl of soup. Every instinct screamed at him to run, yet his hand trembled as he reached for the spoon. The soup smelled exactly like his favorite recipe: warm, comforting, perfect.
He ate slowly, trying to convince himself that this was nothing more than an unusually vivid dream. The black-eyed mother watched him with a soft, unnerving patience, never blinking, never speaking unless he paused too long.
After the meal, Michael wandered through the apartment. Every room looked familiar, yet wrong. The colors were brighter, the corners sharper, the furniture immaculate. No dust, no peeling paint, no crooked picture frames. The apartment breathed, subtly shifting as he moved. Doors seemed slightly taller when he passed through them, hallways stretched and narrowed without warning.
And then he noticed something else: nothing here cast a reflection except him. Mirrors showed his own face perfectly, but the black-eyed woman never appeared. Only the faintest shimmer in the corner, like a ripple in air, hinted at her presence.
Michael’s stomach twisted. The world was perfect. Too perfect. And that perfection made it feel wrong.
He moved toward the window. Outside was snow — soft, endless, and unbroken. But the snow didn’t fall naturally. It floated in slow, deliberate arcs, like someone had painted it into existence. The sky was pale, almost white, with no hint of clouds or sun. It didn’t feel cold; it felt… empty.
“Michael,” the voice came again, soft and warm, drifting from somewhere behind him. He spun. She stood at the doorway, hands folded neatly, black eyes glinting.
“You don’t have to worry,” she said. “You have everything you’ve ever wanted. You’re safe here. You’re loved.”
He shook his head. “It’s not real,” he said, though his voice sounded small even to him.
“It’s real enough,” she replied. “And it could be more real. You could have it all… if you just stay.”
Michael took a step back. The apartment seemed to pulse with her words. The carpet beneath his feet shifted like a living thing. The walls stretched slightly as though leaning in to hear him breathe.
He ran a hand along the railing of the staircase, and it quivered under his fingers, alive with some strange, patient energy. Every instinct in his body screamed to leave, yet every comforting detail—the smell of soup, the warmth of the radiator, the quiet hum of the walls—pulled him in deeper.
He explored room after room, testing doors that led only to more rooms like the ones he had left. Every corridor curved slightly wrong, hallways folded over themselves, and the soft hum followed him wherever he went.
And then he found it. A small, wooden music box on a side table. He recognized the tune before the lid even opened — his father’s favorite song, the one he hadn’t heard since childhood.
The box spun and played, filling the apartment with delicate notes. Michael’s chest tightened. It wasn’t just a memory being recreated; it felt alive, pressing in on him, as if the music itself knew him.
And somewhere, somewhere very close, he thought he heard a faint whisper:
“You can have everything… if you belong to me.”
Michael froze. The music stopped. The snow outside ceased its slow fall. The hum from the walls lingered, patient and insistent.
He swallowed hard. He realized what he already feared: this place was perfect, but it was alive. And it had noticed.

























Chapter 6 – The Gift
Michael didn’t sleep that night. He lay on the soft carpet of the other apartment, staring at the ceiling, listening to the faint hum of the walls. Every so often, a shadow shifted across the room, though he saw nothing move. His stomach twisted with a mixture of fear and longing.
Morning came, though the light was strange — pale, silver, impossible. On the table, he found a small wooden box he hadn’t seen before. It was smooth, polished, and carefully carved with tiny swirling patterns. He hesitated before lifting the lid.
Inside was a book. The leather cover was warm to the touch. The title glimmered faintly: Stories You’ve Always Wanted to Read.
He opened it. The pages shimmered like silk. Words crawled across the paper, rearranging themselves into sentences he hadn’t written, sentences he wanted to read. The book seemed alive, almost whispering in his mind.
A voice floated across the room: “It is yours.”
Michael looked up. She was there again — the woman who looked like his mother. Her black eyes caught the light, reflecting it like polished stone. She held another small object behind her back.
“Here,” she said, placing a mirror beside the book. It was small, round, with a silver frame. When Michael glanced into it, he saw himself… smiling. Not the tired, wary smile he wore every day, but a full, untroubled grin.
“This mirror shows you as you could be,” she explained softly. “You’re happy here. You’re safe here. Everything that makes you uneasy in the other world, it disappears here. Everything you want is yours.”
Michael’s stomach knotted. He wanted to reach out, touch the book, peer into the mirror, let himself believe it. For a moment, the pull was almost unbearable. This world offered everything he had been missing: warmth, safety, attention, comfort.
And yet, something in the hum of the walls, something in the too-perfect way the light fell, set his nerves on edge.
He backed away. “It’s… it’s not real,” he whispered.
She smiled, tilting her head like a cat. “Real enough, Michael. Real enough for anyone who wants it.”
Her voice changed then — just slightly, almost imperceptibly. Beneath the sweetness was patience… and a faint edge of something sharper. It lingered like a shadow in the corner of the room.
Michael’s heart raced. He realized that everything she offered — the book, the mirror, the soup — was a test. Not just a temptation, but a measure. The closer he leaned in, the more it tried to wrap him in comfort.
He glanced toward the hallway, toward the other rooms. They stretched endlessly, folding in on themselves, walls bending, floors soft and pulsing beneath his feet. Every corner seemed alive, watching, waiting.
And somewhere beneath it all, the hum grew louder, insistent.
“Everything is yours… if you stay… if you belong to me…”
Michael felt a shiver run through him. The book, the mirror, the food — all of it was a trap, wrapped in comfort, beautiful and terrifying all at once.
He wanted to run, but he didn’t know which way. The apartment, the rooms, even the hallways were alive, almost breathing. The pull of warmth and familiarity tugged at him, whispering that everything would be easier if he stayed.
But he knew he couldn’t.
He had to get out.


























Chapter 7 – The Sewing Box
Michael woke to a strange silence. The usual hum of the walls was gone, leaving the apartment unnervingly still. Even the soft glow of the windows felt muted, as if the world itself were holding its breath.
On the table in the center of the room sat a small wooden box. It was polished, carved with intricate spirals, and smaller than the box that had held the book and mirror. He didn’t remember seeing it before. His stomach tightened.
He reached for it hesitantly. Inside lay two black buttons and a long needle threaded with dark silk. The silk shimmered faintly, almost alive, as if it were moving of its own accord.
From behind him came the voice — soft, calm, almost comforting:
“Michael,” she said. He turned slowly. The woman who looked like his mother stood in the doorway, hands folded, black eyes glinting. “You’ve been so good to explore. So patient. But it’s time to belong. Just one thing — this is all it will take.”
He swallowed. His fingers trembled. “Belong…? I don’t understand.”
She stepped closer, holding out her hands as if offering a gift. “You see, my boy… everything you want, everything you’ve ever missed… it’s here. You could have it all. Warmth, safety, happiness. You could stay forever. All you need… is a little stitch.”
Michael looked down at the needle and buttons. He understood instantly — it wasn’t just sewing. It was a mark, a way to bind him here, to make him hers.
“I… I don’t want it,” he said, voice shaking.
Her smile faltered, just for a fraction of a second, before smoothing back into the same soft, calm mask. But the air in the room shifted. The walls seemed to pulse beneath his feet. The hum returned, deeper, insistent, almost alive.
“Oh, my sweet boy,” she whispered. “You’ve already agreed by coming here. You’re mine… whether you like it or not.”
Michael’s chest tightened. Fear, sharp and icy, surged through him. The walls stretched slightly, shadows bending toward him. The carpet under his knees seemed soft, pliant, and breathing. Every part of the room felt alive, watching, waiting.
He backed away. The door he had crawled through was gone. Every hallway, every room he had explored twisted in on itself, leaving him trapped in a perfect maze.
His heart pounded. This wasn’t about comfort anymore. It was about control.
And yet… the pull of warmth, of the faint memory of home he had never had, tugged at him, whispering that staying might be easier than fighting.
Michael gritted his teeth. He could feel the walls pulsing beneath his palms. He could hear her voice, sweet and sharp, threading through the hum of the apartment:
“Just one stitch… and you’ll never want for anything again.”
He realized in that moment that she wasn’t just offering him gifts. She was claiming him. And the longer he stayed, the stronger her hold would become.
He couldn’t stay. He had to find the door. Somehow, some way, he had to escape.



Chapter 8 – The Sewing Decision and Escalation
Michael stayed crouched in the corner of the hallway, staring at the small wooden box on the table. The black buttons and needle seemed to pulse faintly in the golden light of the apartment. His stomach churned with fear and something darker — the pull of temptation.
The other mother’s voice drifted softly through the air, almost coaxing:
“It’s only one stitch, Michael. One small stitch, and you will never be cold, never be alone, never be hungry again.”
Michael swallowed hard. He knew she was lying — he knew instinctively that this stitch was a trap, a way to make him hers forever. And yet, the comfort she promised tugged at him. The warmth of the apartment, the smell of the perfect soup, the hum of the walls… it was almost intoxicating.
He shook his head violently. “I… I can’t,” he whispered, more to himself than to her.
Her black eyes narrowed slightly, just enough to unsettle him. The soft smile never left her lips, but the air around her seemed to thicken, pressing in. The radiator behind him began to hum louder, as though echoing her impatience.
Michael felt the walls of the apartment closing in, subtly, almost imperceptibly. Each corner seemed sharper, the carpet softer, the light warmer — the entire space urging him to give in. He realized, with a shiver, that the apartment itself was part of her. It responded to him, bending and breathing to coax him into staying.
He glanced around desperately. There had to be a way out. But every doorway led only to more of the same immaculate rooms, the same impossible perfection. Even the hallways seemed to curve inward, subtly reshaping to contain him.
And still she spoke, her voice calm, gentle, relentless:
“You don’t have to fight. You could have everything you’ve ever wanted. Just one stitch, and all of this is yours.”
Michael’s hands shook. He could feel the pull of the silk needle in his mind, the imagined warmth of the stitch binding him to this world. He had to resist. If he gave in, there would be no going back.
He backed toward the hallway, breathing hard, forcing himself to focus. There has to be a way out, he thought. There must be a door. There must be a crack.
And then he noticed it: a faint glow behind the radiator. Not the same as the rest of the walls, but a soft, cool light, pulsing faintly. His heart raced. The outline of the small door appeared, shimmering slightly, as if acknowledging his presence.
Michael’s hands clenched into fists. He didn’t dare turn his back on the other mother, whose voice now had an edge of impatience, almost anger:
“Michael… don’t make this harder than it needs to be.”
He didn’t respond. He didn’t move. He had made his decision. He was going to escape — no stitch, no compromise. He was going to leave this perfect, impossible apartment and return to his cold, imperfect world.
And he would fight to make it back.



Chapter 9 – The Escape
Michael’s heart pounded so hard it seemed to echo in the walls. The hum of the apartment had grown into a deep, steady thrum, vibrating through the floor, the ceiling, even the air around him. Every part of the space pressed in on him, alive and aware.
The black-eyed woman stepped forward, her smile calm but now impossibly wide, her voice a silky hiss:
“Michael… you can’t leave. You belong here. You’ve always belonged.”
He swallowed, forcing his fear into sharp focus. One step at a time, he edged toward the faint glow behind the radiator. The small door pulsed softly, cool light spilling around its edges. It was his only chance.
Her presence seemed to warp the space around him. Hallways stretched and twisted, the ceiling rising impossibly high before compressing again, just enough to disorient him. He stumbled, pressed himself against the wall, and forced himself to keep moving forward.
“Michael…” she called again, softer this time, coaxing him. But now there was anger in the undercurrent of her voice, like a predator frustrated at its prey. “You cannot resist me forever.”
He didn’t stop. His hands found the edge of the small door. It was cold, almost alive, pulsing faintly under his touch. He yanked it open. The tunnel beyond shimmered with a cool, faint light — the same air he remembered from when he first crawled in. Relief flooded through him, but he didn’t dare pause.
Behind him, the apartment seemed to convulse. Walls stretched, floors rippled, light bending unnaturally. A whisper surged through the air, loud, insistent, as if the building itself were screaming:
“Michael… come back… it’s perfect here…”
He ignored it. He crawled into the tunnel, scraping his palms along the slick black bricks. The space constricted and widened in impossible ways, almost as if it were alive and trying to trap him.
A sudden, violent shudder ran through the tunnel. Dust and flakes of plaster rained down. Michael scrambled forward, gagging against the thick air, ignoring the pain in his hands and knees.
Finally, after what felt like hours though could only have been minutes, he spotted a familiar light — the dim gray of his real hallway. He pushed forward with everything he had left, dragging his body out onto the floor of Apartment 47.
The door slammed shut behind him with a hiss. Silence followed. The apartment was cold and gray, just as it had been before. The hum of the walls was gone. His hands were scraped and raw, his breath ragged, but he was free.
Michael collapsed on the carpet, shivering, heart still racing. The radiator clanged faintly above him, ordinary once more. The small seam in the wall was gone — smooth plaster where the door had been. It was as if it had never existed.
When his mother returned late that night, exhausted from her shift, Michael sat quietly, pale and still. She asked him what was wrong, and he only shook his head.
“Old pipes,” he said finally. “They talk sometimes.”
He didn’t lie — not entirely.
And yet, when he lay down that night, the hum returned — faint, distant, patient. Somewhere behind the walls, a soft whisper carried:
“Come back, my boy… it’s so much warmer here…”
Michael clutched the butter knife he had hidden under his pillow. He didn’t sleep, but he survived.
The world he knew was imperfect, cold, lonely. But it was real. And he would never crawl through that door again.






































Extra – The Whispering Walls
Weeks had passed since Michael escaped. Winter still clung to Saint Petersburg, gray and stubborn, and the snow outside lay thick on the streets and roofs. The world seemed smaller now, colder, but somehow more real.
Michael moved through his apartment quietly, noticing every crack in the plaster, every peeling patch of wallpaper, every uneven floorboard. He no longer longed for perfection — he had seen what “perfect” could demand, and it had almost claimed him.
The radiator hummed faintly as it always did, just a regular, tired hiss this time. Michael leaned against it, listening, testing it. Nothing unnatural. No soft coaxing, no gentle pull of warmth.
He had repaired the tiny cut on his palm from crawling through the tunnel, and the bruises on his knees had faded, but the memory of the other world remained vivid in his mind. Every shadow seemed sharper now, every whisper of wind through the walls carrying a hint of… something waiting.
Michael’s mother still came home late from work. He still ate the same bland soup, still lived in a cramped apartment. And yet everything felt different. He noticed, now, the small details he had once overlooked — the warmth of sunlight spilling through the window, the faint squeak of the stairwell, the way the wind rattled the old radiator covers. Life was imperfect. But it was alive.
At night, when he lay in bed, he sometimes imagined the hum returning. Just a faint echo, distant, patient, waiting behind the walls. A soft whisper teased the edges of his mind:
“Come back, Michael… it’s warmer here…”
He gripped the butter knife under his pillow, fingers tightening around it, and closed his eyes.
“No,” he whispered into the dark. “I belong here. Not there.”
The hum faded once more. He felt the chill of the apartment, the rough comfort of his reality. He wasn’t perfect. He was bruised, frightened, and cautious. But he was free.
And that was enough.
Michael didn’t sleep that night. He didn’t need to. He simply listened, awake, alive, and waiting for the winter to end.
The other world was still out there, behind the walls, patient. But Michael had learned something essential: some doors, no matter how tempting, are meant to stay closed.
